# Russia's Drone War Spills Into NATO Territory

**BUCHAREST** — Residents of a Romanian city are returning to check on their homes after a Russian drone struck an apartment block early Friday morning, sparking a fire and injuring two people, according to Romanian officials.

The strike marks one of the closest incursions into NATO territory since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine began. Romania, a member of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization, has long been on the front lines of the geopolitical fallout from the war — but this is the first time a Russian drone has directly hit a residential building on Romanian soil.

"'No-one feels safe now,'" one resident told the BBC, speaking on condition of anonymity. "We came back to see what's left."

## A Delicate Line Crossed

The drone attack comes as US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth was questioned at an Asian security summit in Singapore about America's commitment to Asia-Pacific allies — a conversation that suddenly feels sharper given events a continent away.

Romanian authorities say the drone appears to have been targeting infrastructure in neighboring Ukraine, but veered off course. Officials in Kyiv have not yet commented on the incident.

Under NATO's collective defense clause, an armed attack on one member is considered an attack on all. But so far, the alliance has stopped short of directly interpreting incidents like this as grounds for Article 5 invocation, instead providing air defense support and surveillance to border states.

That careful line is getting harder to hold.

## Residents Caught in the Middle

The impact happened in the early hours, when most residents were asleep. Two people sustained injuries and were taken to local hospitals. The building's top floors bore the worst of the damage — windows blown out, balconies warped by heat.

A 70-year-old widow told the BBC the inability to use her building's elevator during a power outage trapped her and her husband when he needed medical care. For her, the drone strike is just the latest chapter in a months-long ordeal of uncertainty.

Power outages have plagued the region since cross-border strikes began increasing in frequency. The woman's building had already been without consistent electricity for weeks.

## International Response

Romania's president convened an emergency meeting of the country's Supreme Defence Council shortly after the strike. NATO has dispatched additional surveillance assets to the Black Sea region, according to two alliance officials who spoke on background.

The incident is likely to intensify debates within the alliance about whether existing rules of engagement are sufficient — and whether the definition of "direct attack" needs to be revisited.

For now, residents of the hit apartment block are left piecing together what remains of their homes, wondering if the next strike will land closer — or next time, come with a different answer from NATO.
