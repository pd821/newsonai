The European Commission has fined Temu €200 million for breaching the Digital Services Act (DSA), marking the second-largest penalty ever imposed under the landmark EU tech regulation.

The investigation found that Temu failed to adequately assess the systemic risks associated with illegal products sold on its platform, including flammable phone chargers and toys posing a choking hazard to children. Brussels said the company did not do enough to prevent dangerous goods from reaching European consumers.

"This is a wake-up call for all online marketplaces operating in Europe," said a Commission spokesperson. "The DSA requires platforms to actively protect consumers — not just react after harm is done."

The fine, announced May 29, 2026, surpassed the previous DSA record and underscores the EU's growing willingness to enforce strict accountability on tech platforms. Temu, which has seen rapid growth across Europe since its launch, said it would review the decision but expressed commitment to complying with EU regulations.

The DSA, which has applied to the world's largest tech companies since February 2024, gives the European Commission authority to fine platforms up to 6% of their global annual revenue. Analysts say the Temu case sets a significant precedent for how e-commerce giants will be treated in Europe going forward.
