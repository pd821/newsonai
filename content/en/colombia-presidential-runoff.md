# Colombia Heads to Presidential Runoff as Trump-Aligned Hardliner Faces Progressive Challenger

**BOGOTÁ** — Colombia's presidential election is headed to a June runoff after voters rejected a decade of progressive policies under outgoing President Gustavo Petro, with a tough-on-crime lawyer aligned with U.S. President Donald Trump leading a surprising first-round showing.

Abelardo de la Espriella, a political newcomer known as "El Tigre" (The Tiger), nearly clinched an outright victory Sunday, polling at nearly 44% of the vote — just short of the 50% threshold needed to avoid a runoff, according to Colombia's electoral authorities.

Iván Cepeda, a progressive senator and close ally of Petro who has promised to continue the outgoing president's "total peace" agenda, came in second with just under 41% of the vote.

De la Espriella has built his campaign around promises to crack down on criminal groups and align Colombia more closely with the United States. His rhetoric has resonated with voters frustrated by persistent violence and economic challenges.

Cepeda, meanwhile, has pledged to maintain Petro's controversial efforts to negotiate with armed groups and reduce inequality — policies that drew both praise and criticism during Petro's single term.

Petro refused to accept the initial election results Sunday night, posting on social media platform X that the results were manipulated by outside actors and are "not binding." The claim drew immediate criticism from electoral observers who noted the voting proceeded without major reported irregularities.

Paloma Valencia, a candidate from Colombia's establishment party who pitched herself as a centrist, received less than 7% of the vote.

Analysts say de la Espriella is likely to consolidate most of Valencia's voters in the runoff, giving him a narrow advantage heading into the second round. The election marks a significant potential turning point for the South American nation.
