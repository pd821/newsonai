Anthropic has closed a $65 billion Series H funding round, the company announced Thursday, valuing the AI safety-focused lab at $965 billion post-money — a figure that eclipses OpenAI's last reported valuation of $730 billion and marks the largest private funding round in the history of the technology industry.

The round was co-led by Capital Group, Coatue, D1 Capital Partners, GIC, ICONIQ, and XN, withAltimeter Capital, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, and Sequoia Capital taking significant stakes. The roster of investors reads like a who's who of global capital: Blackstone, Brookfield, Fidelity, General Catalyst, Lightspeed Venture Partners, T. Rowe Price, Temasek, and Amazon, Google, and Samsung as strategic partners. The company had previously announced $15 billion in committed hyperscaler investment, including $5 billion from Amazon.

"This funding will help us serve the historic demand we are experiencing, stay at the research frontier, and bring Claude to more of the places where work happens," said Krishna Rao, Anthropic's Chief Financial Officer, in a statement.

## The Number That Stands Out

The $965 billion valuation is the headline. But the revenue trajectory is what gives it context. Anthropic said its run-rate revenue crossed $47 billion earlier this month — a figure that would place it among the fastest-growing enterprise software companies in history, if not the fastest. The company crossed $47 billion in annualized revenue less than two years after its Series G at a $61.5 billion valuation.

For comparison: OpenAI's most recent valuation was $730 billion. Anthropic has now surpassed that benchmark by roughly 32 percent. The gap between the two companies that define the frontier AI race has, on this measure, narrowed considerably.

## What the Money Funds

Anthropic has been clear about where the capital is going: safety research, compute expansion, and global product scaling. The company has signed agreements with Amazon for up to five gigawatts of new AI compute capacity, with Google and Broadcom for five gigawatts of next-generation TPU capacity, and with SpaceX for access to GPU capacity in its Colossus 1 and Colossus 2 clusters. Those agreements represent a multi-year infrastructure commitment of a scale that few companies on earth can match.

Strategically, the company has also brought in Micron, Samsung, and SK hynix as infrastructure partners — a deliberate move to secure memory supply as high-bandwidth memory becomes an increasingly constrained resource in AI hardware deployment.

## Claude at Scale

The funding is riding on the back of accelerating enterprise adoption. Anthropic says global enterprises are deploying Claude in core operations, and the company recently upgraded its flagship model family with Claude Opus 4.8 — an update that the company says delivers stronger performance on coding, agentic tasks, and long-running professional work. Claude is now available on all three major cloud platforms: Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure, with AWS remaining its primary training partner.

The company also recently opened a Milan office — its sixth in Europe — and is preparing to open a Seoul office in Korea, signaling a push into continental European and Asian enterprise markets that have been slower to adopt U.S. frontier AI.

## A Defining Moment in the AI Race

The scale of this round reshapes the competitive landscape in ways that go beyond valuation. Anthropic, founded in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers with a stated mission centered on AI safety, has now raised multiple rounds of extraordinary size in rapid succession — a pace that reflects both the appetite of global investors to fund the AI race and the extraordinary capital requirements of building and running frontier models at scale.

The question now is not whether Anthropic can compete at the frontier — the revenue, the model performance, and the infrastructure commitments make that case on their own. The question is whether the company can sustain the pace of capability development, safety research, and enterprise deployment simultaneously while spending at a level that would overwhelm most sovereign wealth funds. The $65 billion says investors believe it can.
