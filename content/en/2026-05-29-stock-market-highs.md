Wall Street ended the week on a high note.

Both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite climbed to fresh all-time highs on May 28, 2026, erasing earlier intraday declines as a cooler-than-expected inflation reading gave investors renewed confidence. The tech-heavy Nasdaq led the rally, while the broader S&P 500 also closed at a record level.

The encouraging inflation data comes amid ongoing concerns: April's PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures) index — the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge — rose 3.8%, marking the highest level in nearly three years. Still, the market's reaction suggested investors had already priced in worse numbers and were relieved by the relative miss versus expectations.

Lower oil prices also contributed to the optimism, easing pressure on consumers and corporate cost structures alike. Analysts noted that despite persistent inflation, the combination of strong corporate earnings and resilient consumer spending has kept the bull market intact.

"Stocks are signaling confidence that the Fed can thread the needle — cooling inflation without triggering a hard landing," said one market strategist. The S&P 500 has now posted its best month since 2020, driven largely by strength in technology and communications sectors.

Investors are now watching next month's jobs data and Fed commentary for clues on whether rate cuts remain on the table for 2026.
