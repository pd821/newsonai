# SoftBank Seals $75 Billion Deal to Build Europe AI Data Center Empire in France

SoftBank has finalized a landmark $75 billion investment agreement to construct a network of AI-optimized data centers across France, in what the company is calling the largest single foreign direct investment in French technology infrastructure history. The deal, negotiated over roughly eight months of talks involving French government officials, EU regulators, and SoftBank's own senior leadership, will establish facilities in at least four French cities with the potential to expand to neighboring markets.

The investment is anchored by SoftBank's AI infrastructure subsidiary, and is widely seen as a bet on Europe's growing demand for localized AI computing capacity — driven by both regulatory pressure to keep European data within European borders and an accelerating enterprise adoption curve that shows no signs of plateauing. France in particular has positioned itself as a preferred destination for AI investment under a government strategy that offers incentives for data center construction in exchange for commitments to local hiring and open-access provisions for research institutions.

The French facilities will be purpose-built for large-scale AI workloads, with a focus on inference and model serving rather than training, reflecting a global trend toward separating those workloads as specialized tasks. Power agreements with Électricité de France will underpin the centers, which SoftBank says will be fully powered by a combination of nuclear and renewable sources — a point designed to address the intense scrutiny data center energy consumption has attracted in European capitals.

SoftBank CEO Masayoshi Son called the agreement "a generational bet on where AI will live" and said Europe had reached an inflection point where scale infrastructure was no longer optional. The company has faced pressure from investors after a series of Vision Fund losses, and the AI infrastructure push is seen internally as a path toward more predictable, asset-heavy revenue streams.

French economic officials welcomed the announcement as a validation of France's strategy to attract advanced computing investment, though labor unions have already flagged concerns about the quality and volume of permanent jobs the facilities will create versus the construction roles they will generate in the near term.
