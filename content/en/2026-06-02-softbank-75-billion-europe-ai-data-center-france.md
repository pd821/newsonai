# SoftBank Pledges €75 Billion to Build Europe's Largest AI Data Center Hub in France

SoftBank has committed up to €75 billion for an artificial intelligence infrastructure project in France that, when complete, would represent the single largest AI data center investment in Europe. The project centers on a 5 gigawatt hub in Dunkirk, with a first phase targeting 3.1 gigawatts by 2031 — enough to power several million homes, or, more likely, several hundred thousand high-density AI server racks.

The announcement signals a dramatic escalation in the race for AI infrastructure sovereignty. For Europe, the project represents a potential leap toward reducing dependence on US cloud providers — AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud collectively dominate European enterprise AI infrastructure. For SoftBank, it's a major bet that physical AI infrastructure is where value will accumulate, much as the company bet on互联网 in the 1990s and移动互联网 a decade later.

## The Numbers Behind the Headline

€75 billion over what timeframe remains deliberately unclear from SoftBank's public statements. The Dunkirk hub alone is projected to require extraordinary amounts of land, power grid capacity, and cooling infrastructure. The 3.1 GW initial phase is approximately equal to the power consumption of a mid-sized European city — all dedicated to running AI training and inference workloads.

France has positioned itself as Europe's most aggressive recruiter of AI infrastructure investment. The country's relatively favorable energy pricing, strong nuclear grid, and permissive permitting environment for large industrial projects have made it a preferred destination for hyperscalers and data center operators. The SoftBank deal would dwarf existing commitments.

## Europe Gets Serious About AI Infrastructure

The investment comes as European policymakers have grown increasingly vocal about the continent's reliance on American and Chinese AI technology. The EU's own AI infrastructure initiatives have struggled to attract comparable capital, partly because private investors see better returns in more established markets.

The political calculus is complicated: large-scale data centers require enormous power, water for cooling, and land. Community opposition — citing environmental concerns and visual impact — has become a significant factor in approval timelines. Sightline Climate analysis suggests that 30-50% of planned US data center projects are facing delays or cancellations for exactly these reasons. France will likely face similar friction even with government support.

SoftBank's involvement also raises questions about Japan's role in global AI governance. SoftBank CEO Masayoshi Son has oscillated between caution and extreme optimism on AI, and this commitment suggests his patience for watching from the sidelines has run out. The company's existing AI-related investments through the Vision Fund portfolio will likely benefit from reduced reliance on third-party cloud infrastructure.

For now, the project moves into a planning phase. Whether the full €75 billion materializes — and on what timeline — will depend heavily on regulatory approvals, power grid agreements, and SoftBank's continued appetite for massive infrastructure bets as the AI landscape evolves.
