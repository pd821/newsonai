# US Expands Nvidia AI Chip Export Controls to Target Chinese Subsidiaries

The United States has announced a significant expansion of its export control policies on AI semiconductors, specifically targeting Chinese subsidiaries of major US technology firms. The new measures represent the most sweeping attempt yet to close what regulators are calling a "subsidiary loophole" — the practice by which Chinese entities access restricted US AI chips through partnerships, joint ventures, or affiliates embedded within the Chinese market.

Under the revised rules, any foreign-produced item that incorporates US-origin AI semiconductors or technology will face equivalent restrictions when destined for end-users in China, regardless of where the chip was manufactured. The policy also introduces new licensing requirements for chips exceeding a defined computational threshold, broadening earlier measures that focused primarily on cutting off direct sales from US companies to Chinese buyers.

The announcement sent ripples through global semiconductor stocks, with Nvidia shares sliding on concerns about revenue exposure in one of the largest AI markets in the world. Intel and Qualcomm both flagged the policy for review, though analysts note that the direct impact on those firms is more limited given their comparatively smaller AI data center presence.

Chinese state media characterized the move as "technological bullying" and hinted at potential retaliatory measures in the rare earth materials sector, a sensitive area for global manufacturing supply chains. Diplomatic channels are expected to carry the friction into already-tentative trade talks slated for later this month.

Industry groups have raised concerns about the operational complexity of the new rules, arguing that tracking chip flows through subsidiaries and joint ventures presents an unprecedented compliance burden. The Commerce Department has signaled a 90-day grace period before enforcement begins, citing the need for companies to restructure supply chains and contracts.

The broader context is the ongoing AI arms race between the US and China, where access to cutting-edge semiconductors is viewed as a decisive strategic advantage. US officials have made clear that maintaining a lead in AI capability — particularly in military and intelligence applications — is a core national security priority. This expansion is the latest in a series of escalations that began with initial export bans in 2022 and have grown progressively more comprehensive.

For enterprises on all sides, the episode underscores how semiconductor policy has become inseparable from AI strategy. The era of treating chip access as a purely commercial question is over.
