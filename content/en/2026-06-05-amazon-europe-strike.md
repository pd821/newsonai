# Amazon Workers in Germany, Spain, and Italy Begin Largest-Ever European Strike

*25,000 workers demand 15% wage increase as strike coincides with Prime Day*

BERLIN — Over 25,000 Amazon workers across Germany, Spain, and Italy launched a coordinated three-day strike Thursday, demanding a 15% wage increase and improved safety conditions, in what labor organizers call the largest-ever European action against the e-commerce giant.

The strike coincides with Amazon's annual Prime Day event, potentially affecting millions of deliveries across the continent. Verdi, Germany's largest trade union, is leading coordination efforts, with support from CC.OO in Spain and CGIL in Italy.

Workers at Amazon's massive fulfillment centers—warehouse facilities that can span the size of several football fields—say they face grueling conditions including mandatory overtime, productivity surveillance via wristband tracking, and temperatures that can exceed 30 degrees Celsius in summer without adequate cooling.

"Amazon makes billions while we risk our health," said Maria Hoffmann, a picker at Amazon's Leipzig fulfillment center. "We are not asking for charity. We are asking for dignity."

Amazon disputed the union's characterization of working conditions, saying its average hourly wage in Germany is €20.50 ($22.30)—significantly above the minimum wage—and that its safety record is among the best in the logistics industry.

"Amazon remains committed to being a good employer," said spokesperson Jason Krolner. "We offer industry-leading pay, comprehensive benefits, and opportunities for career growth. We respect our employees' right to strike but believe the overwhelming majority of our workers prefer to build their careers with us."

The timing is particularly painful for Amazon commercially. Prime Day typically generates more than $10 billion in sales over 48 hours, and any significant disruption to fulfillment could delay millions of packages, potentially affecting Amazon's ability to meet its delivery promises.

German retail analysts estimated that if the strike extends through the full three days, Amazon could face delivery delays affecting 2-3 million packages in Germany alone. The company has activated contingency measures, including temporary staffing from third-party logistics providers.

Financial markets reacted minimally to the news, with Amazon shares trading slightly lower in Thursday afternoon session.
