The tech industry's layoff streak shows no signs of slowing.

May 2026 has become one of the bloodiest months for technology workers in recent memory, with roughly 28,000 employees laid off across major companies including Meta, PayPal, Cisco, Intuit, LinkedIn, Cloudflare, and Wix — all within a single month, according to layoff tracking site Layoff.fyi.

Meta led the pack, cutting approximately 8,000 employees — about 10% of its global workforce — while simultaneously shifting over 7,000 workers into AI-focused projects. PayPal announced 4,760 cuts and signaled plans to reduce its headcount by up to 20% over the next two to three years as it expands AI efforts. Cisco removed over 4,000 roles, Intuit cut 3,000 (17% of its staff), and LinkedIn let go of 875 employees.

In almost every case, company leadership pointed to artificial intelligence as a key driver. Cisco CEO Chuck Robbins put it plainly: "The companies that will win in the AI era will be those with focus, urgency, and the discipline to continuously shift investment toward areas where demand and long-term value creation are strongest."

The cuts bring the year-to-date total for tech sector layoffs to over 100,000 — a figure that has alarmed labor advocates and economists who warn that AI-driven displacement is accelerating faster than new job creation can compensate.

Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah recently warned that AI will displace human labor at large scale, a sentiment that is increasingly reflected in corporate restructuring decisions across the industry.
