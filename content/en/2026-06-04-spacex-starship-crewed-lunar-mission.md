# SpaceX Starship Completes First Crewed Lunar Orbital Mission

*June 4, 2026 — SpaceX's Starship has successfully completed its first crewed mission to lunar orbit, carrying four astronauts in a historic milestone for commercial spaceflight.*

## What Happened

SpaceX's Starship spacecraft completed its first crewed lunar orbital mission on June 4, 2026, successfully returning four astronauts to Earth after an eight-day mission. The achievement marks the first time a commercial spacecraft has carried humans beyond low Earth orbit.

The crew — two NASA astronauts and two SpaceX test pilots — orbited the Moon at a closest approach of 100 kilometers before beginning the return journey. The mission, designated Starship L1, proceeded without major incidents.

## Key Points

- **First crewed lunar mission by a commercial spacecraft**
- **Mission duration**: 8 days, with 3 days in lunar orbit
- **Crew size**: 4 astronauts (2 NASA, 2 SpaceX)
- **Closest approach**: 100 km above lunar surface
- **Spacecraft condition**: No significant damage reported during reentry

## Why It Matters

The success validates SpaceX's approach to building a fully reusable spacecraft capable of deep space missions. Starship is now the largest and most powerful spacecraft ever to carry humans, surpassing NASA's Saturn V rocket in terms of payload capacity.

"This proves commercial spacecraft can do what only governments could before," said SpaceX CEO Elon Musk. "We're on our way to making life multiplanetary."

## NASA Artemis Connection

The Starship L1 mission is closely tied to NASA's Artemis program. NASA has contracted SpaceX's Starship as the human landing system for Artemis missions that would put astronauts on the Moon. This crewed orbital test provides critical data ahead of the planned 2027 lunar landing mission.

## What's Next

SpaceX plans two additional Starship crewed missions before the Artemis III lunar landing:
- **Starship L2** (late 2026): Extended lunar orbit mission with six crew members
- **Starship L3** (mid-2027): Full dress rehearsal for lunar landing
