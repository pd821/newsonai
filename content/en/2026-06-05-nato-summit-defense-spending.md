# NATO Summit Concludes with Historic Defense Spending Agreement

*NATO allies commit to 3% GDP defense spending in landmark Brussels deal*

BRUSSELS — NATO leaders concluded a two-day summit in Brussels on Thursday with a landmark agreement committing all 32 member states to a minimum 3% GDP defense spending target—a significant increase from the previous 2% benchmark that had proven controversial among European allies.

The deal also includes new cyber defense protocols, expanded joint military exercises in Eastern Europe, and a coordinated weapons procurement framework aimed at reducing duplication and strengthening industrial base cooperation.

"This is a new era for NATO," said NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte at a post-summit press conference. "For the first time, we have universal, binding commitments that reflect the realities of today's security environment."

The summit, held against the backdrop of ongoing tensions in Ukraine and uncertainty over the future of American security guarantees, saw notably strong participation from European leaders who have long resisted higher defense spending targets.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, who championed the agreement domestically, called it "a historic investment in our collective security" and confirmed Germany would exceed the 3% target through its recently announced special defense fund.

French President Emmanuel Macron praised the agreement while emphasizing the need for European strategic autonomy. "NATO remains the cornerstone of European defense," he said, "but we must also build the capacity to act independently when our interests require it."

The new spending target replaces a 2014 Wales commitment that allowed countries to "aim for" 2%—a formulation that critics said allowed members to fall short without consequences. Under the new framework, members who consistently fail to meet commitments will face formal review at the heads of state level.

Analysts noted the agreement marks a significant shift in European defense posture. "What we're seeing is a structural transformation of European security," said Dr. Helena Evans, senior fellow at the European Council on Foreign Relations. "The 2% debate is over. Now the question is how fast members can actually build the capabilities they've promised."

The summit also launched a new NATO Innovation Fund, allocating €1 billion over five years to support dual-use technology development, including artificial intelligence applications for defense and advanced manufacturing capabilities.

All 32 allied leaders signed the summit communiqué, which also addressed threats in the Indo-Pacific region and reaffirmed NATO's open-door policy for future enlargement.
