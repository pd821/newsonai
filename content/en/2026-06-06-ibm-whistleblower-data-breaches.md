# Former Cybersecurity Executive Alleges IBM Covered Up Multiple Data Breaches

*Whistleblower lawsuit claims IBM and subsidiaries were breached in mid-2010s, with company actively concealing the incidents*

**June 6, 2026** — A former cybersecurity executive has turned whistleblower, accusing IBM of covering up several data breaches that occurred during the mid-2010s, according to a TechCrunch report published Friday.

The lawsuit, filed by the former executive who previously held a senior cybersecurity role at IBM, alleges that the company and two of its subsidiary companies were breached during that period — and that IBM not only failed to disclose the incidents but actively worked to conceal them.

## Detailed Allegations

The whistleblower claims the breaches involved sensitive corporate and customer data, with the incidents remaining undisclosed for years. The former executive's legal action seeks to hold IBM accountable for what the lawsuit describes as a systematic failure to protect data and notify affected parties.

TechCrunch reports that the breaches have also drawn attention from federal investigators, who are examining whether IBM's handling of the incidents violated data breach notification laws.

## Corporate Response

IBM has denied the allegations, stating that the company takes security seriously and has robust protocols in place for addressing and disclosing security incidents. The company declined to comment on specific allegations, citing ongoing litigation.

## Security Industry Implications

The case highlights ongoing tensions between corporate security practices and disclosure requirements. Critics have long argued that companies face perverse incentives to minimize or conceal breaches, particularly when the full scope of damage may not be immediately apparent.

"Until there's real accountability for concealment, companies will continue to weigh the reputational cost of disclosure against the legal risk," said one cybersecurity attorney not involved in the case. "That's exactly the wrong calculation."

## What's Next

The lawsuit is expected to proceed through discovery, which will likely reveal more details about the alleged breaches and IBM's response. Legal experts say the case could set precedents for how data breach allegations are handled in corporate litigation.
