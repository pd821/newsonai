# US Expands Nvidia AI Chip Export Controls to 12 Additional Countries

*Nvidia estimates $15 billion in annual sales could be affected by new restrictions*

WASHINGTON — The Biden administration on Thursday expanded its sweeping AI chip export controls to 12 additional countries in Southeast Asia and the Middle East, citing national security concerns over Beijing's access to advanced computing capabilities.

The new restrictions target countries including Vietnam, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates, requiring licenses for exports of advanced AI semiconductors to entities in those markets. China-linked entities across all covered regions face complete export bans, according to rules released by the Commerce Department.

Nvidia, the leading designer of AI accelerator chips, estimated the new restrictions could affect up to $15 billion in annual sales. The company's shares fell 4.2% in after-hours trading following the announcement.

"This is about ensuring advanced American AI capabilities don't end up strengthening adversarial systems," said Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo. "We are laser-focused on preventing China from accessing the computing power it needs to develop military AI applications."

The rules expand on restrictions first implemented in 2022 that targeted China directly. Under the new framework, chips with performance levels above specified thresholds will require government approval for export to a broader set of markets deemed at risk of diversion to China.

The Semiconductor Industry Association expressed concern about the expansion, saying it "captures legitimate commercial transactions with allied nations" and could push affected countries toward Chinese alternatives. "The rules need a surgical approach, not a broad brush," said the association's president.

Regional analysts noted the timing could complicate diplomatic relations. Indonesia and Vietnam have both sought to maintain balanced relationships with both Washington and Beijing, and the new rules may intensify pressure to choose sides.

The restrictions come as AI chip demand continues to dramatically outpace supply, with analysts projecting the market for advanced AI accelerators will exceed $400 billion annually by 2028. Nvidia's H100 and newer Blackwell architecture chips remain the industry standard for training large AI models.
