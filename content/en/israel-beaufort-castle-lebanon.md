# Israel Captures Strategic Beaufort Castle in Deepest Lebanon Incursion in Over Two Decades

**BEIRUT** — Israeli troops have captured a strategic mountain topped with a Crusader-built castle in southern Lebanon, marking the deepest incursion into the country in more than a quarter-century, the Israeli military confirmed Sunday.

The taking of Beaufort castle, near the city of Nabatiyeh, followed days of intense airstrikes and fighting in nearby villages between Israeli forces and Hezbollah militants.

The capture represents a significant Israeli advance in the latest chapter of the Israel-Hezbollah conflict, which reignited on March 2 when Hezbollah fired rockets into northern Israel — two days after the United States and Israel launched attacks on Iran, the militant group's main backer.

Since then, Israel has launched a ground invasion capturing dozens of Lebanese villages and towns close to the border. Hezbollah has launched thousands of missiles and drones at Israeli soldiers stationed in southern Lebanon and northern Israel.

The offensive comes despite a nominal ceasefire that has been in place since April 17, and just days before Lebanon and Israel are scheduled to hold another round of direct negotiations in Washington starting Tuesday.

Lebanese Parliament Speaker Nabih Berri, a key Hezbollah ally, said he can guarantee the militant group's "full, comprehensive and immediate commitment to a ceasefire." However, Israeli operations have continued to advance.

Beaufort castle, a Crusader-built fortress dating back to medieval times, has changed hands before — it was held by Israeli forces until 2000, when they withdrew from Lebanon under international pressure. The strategic elevation provided watchers a clear vantage over wide swaths of southern Lebanon.

The latest escalation represents one of the most significant shifts in the region since the Gaza conflict began, drawing concerns from international observers about the prospect of broader regional warfare.
