# WHO Declares Global Health Emergency Over Mpox Variant in Africa

*June 4, 2026 — The World Health Organization has declared a global health emergency following the rapid spread of a new mpox variant in Central and East Africa, citing higher transmissibility than previous strains.*

## What Happened

The World Health Organization declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) on June 4, 2026, after a new mpox variant spread rapidly across Central and East Africa. The variant — designated clade Ib — shows higher transmissibility than previous strains and has caused a significant increase in cases since April.

As of June 4, more than 15,000 confirmed cases and 450 deaths have been reported across 12 countries, with the majority in the Democratic Republic of Congo, Uganda, and Kenya.

## Key Points

- **Variant**: Clade Ib — more transmissible than previous strains
- **Cases**: 15,000+ confirmed across 12 countries
- **Deaths**: 450+ reported
- **Primary regions**: Democratic Republic of Congo, Uganda, Kenya
- **Symptoms**: Similar to previous mpox strains but with potentially higher severity in immunocompromised individuals

## Why It Matters

WHO declarations trigger international response mechanisms, including accelerated vaccine distribution, funding commitments, and enhanced border surveillance. This is the second mpox-related global health emergency declared by WHO — the first was in 2022 when clade II spread globally.

The clade Ib variant appears to spread more easily through non-sexual contact, including household transmission, raising concerns about community spread beyond initial outbreak areas.

## Global Response

**Vaccine Distribution**: WHO has authorized the release of emergency stockpiles from the G7 nations, with 2 million doses to be distributed to affected countries within 30 days.

**Research**: WHO has prioritized research into the clade Ib variant's transmissibility and vaccine efficacy against the new strain.

**Travel Advisories**: Several countries have issued travel advisories for affected regions, though WHO has not recommended travel restrictions.

## What This Means for Global Health

Health experts have expressed concern that the international community's slow response to earlier warning signs contributed to the current outbreak's scale. This declaration is intended to mobilize resources more rapidly than in previous outbreaks.
