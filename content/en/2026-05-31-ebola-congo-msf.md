# Ebola Outbreak in DR Congo Called "Deeply Alarming" by MSF

**GOMA, DR CONGO** — The international medical charity Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) has issued a stark warning about the rapid spread of Ebola in the Democratic Republic of Congo, describing the outbreak as "deeply alarming" as the head of the World Health Organization visits the worst-affected region.

The warning comes amid reports that the virus is spreading faster than health workers can contain it. Transmission chains are extending into urban centers and areas that had previously been considered low-risk.

## WHO Chief on the Ground

WHO Director-General Dr. Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus arrived in the region Friday, meeting with local health officials and touring treatment centers that are operating at or near capacity. His visit is intended to galvanize international support — but critics say the global response has been too slow.

"This is exactly the kind of outbreak that should trigger immediate, massive international attention," said Dr. Isabelle Moreau, MSF's emergency coordinator in the region. "Instead, we're watching resources trickle in while the virus spreads."

## A Familiar Battle

The DRC has experienced multiple Ebola outbreaks over the past decade — including one that killed more than 2,000 people between 2018 and 2020. Health workers have learned valuable lessons about containment, but each new outbreak presents unique challenges: remote forest communities, weak infrastructure, armed conflict in the east, and deep public mistrust of foreign medical teams.

This time around, officials say the strain is a less-common variant, and early symptoms can be mistaken for malaria or typhoid — delaying diagnosis and increasing the window for transmission.

## What's Being Done

The WHO has released emergency funding and deployed additional epidemiologists to the region. Several neighboring countries have begun screening travelers at border crossings. The DRC government has launched a public awareness campaign, though misinformation about the virus — and about vaccines — remains widespread on social media.

MSF is calling for a rapid scale-up of community engagement, not just clinical resources. "You can't treat your way out of an Ebola outbreak," Dr. Moreau said. "You have to work with communities from day one."
