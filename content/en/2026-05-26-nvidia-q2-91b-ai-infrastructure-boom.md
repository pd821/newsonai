# Nvidia Lifts Q2 Guidance to $91B, Signals AI Infrastructure Boom Is Accelerating

Nvidia forecast Q2 revenue of $91 billion — roughly 12 percent above what analysts had expected — sending a clear signal that the AI factory buildout is not slowing down. The company's Q1 FY2027 results, released Thursday for the quarter ended April 26, 2026, showed $81.6 billion in total revenue, up 85 percent year over year, and Nvidia's chief executive Jensen Huang framed the moment as evidence of a transformation that is still in its early innings.

"The buildout of AI factories — the largest infrastructure expansion in human history — is accelerating at extraordinary speed," Huang said on the earnings call.

## Guidance Beat

Wall Street had modeled around $81 billion for Q2. Nvidia guided to $91 billion, plus or minus 2 percent. The gap drew immediate attention. Analysts have spent months debating whether hyperscalers and enterprises were nearing a plateau in AI capital spending after two years of explosive GPU procurement. Thursday's guidance suggests they are not. Customers appear to be committing to multi-year infrastructure rolls that show no obvious ceiling in the near term.

Revenue for Q1 came in at $81.6 billion, a 20 percent sequential jump and an 85 percent increase from the same quarter a year ago. The Data Center segment — Nvidia's dominant business — hit a record $75.2 billion, up 92 percent year over year and 21 percent sequentially.

## Inside the Data Center Numbers

The segment breaks into two large categories. Data Center compute — GPUs, CPUs, and related hardware for AI training and inference — drove $60.4 billion, a record and a 77 percent year-over-year gain. Networking, led by InfiniBand and Ethernet switches, posted $14.8 billion, up a remarkable 199 percent year over year and 35 percent sequentially. The networking growth is notable: as AI clusters scale to tens of thousands of GPUs, interconnect bandwidth becomes a bottleneck, and Nvidia's Mellanox division has become central to that story.

Nvidia also introduced a revised reporting structure this quarter, separating Data Center into Hyperscale and ACIE (a new enterprise/edge category) while listing Edge Computing separately. The shift reflects how AI demand is broadening beyond the large cloud providers into industrial, telecom, and edge deployments.

## Margins Hold, Earnings Surge

GAAP gross margin came in at 74.9 percent; non-GAAP at 75.0 percent. These figures are roughly in line with recent quarters, suggesting that while GPU pricing power remains strong, the company is not seeing major margin expansion — a sign that costs are rising in step with revenue. GAAP net income reached $58.3 billion, up 211 percent year over year. GAAP earnings per share were $2.39, up 214 percent; non-GAAP EPS hit $1.87, up 140 percent.

## Capital Returns and the Balance Sheet

Nvidia returned approximately $20 billion to shareholders in Q1 through buybacks and dividends. On May 18, the board authorized an additional $80 billion share repurchase program and raised the quarterly dividend from 1 cent to 25 cents per share. The dividend increase is a structural signal — Nvidia is generating far more cash than it needs for operations and is returning much of it to investors.

## "Agentic AI Has Arrived"

Huang was direct in characterizing the current moment: "Agentic AI has arrived, doing productive work, generating real value and scaling rapidly across companies and industries." Agentic AI refers to systems that autonomously plan and execute multi-step tasks — a capability that requires dense, expensive compute infrastructure. If the thesis holds, it means the next wave of AI spending will not be about training foundation models alone but about deploying agents at scale across enterprise workflows, each requiring persistent, low-latency compute.

"NVIDIA is uniquely positioned at the center of this transformation," Huang added, "as the only platform that runs in every cloud, powers every frontier and open source model, and scales everywhere AI is produced — from hyperscale data centers to the edge."

## What Comes Next

The Q2 guidance implies Nvidia will post roughly $93 billion in revenue if it hits the midpoint — a figure that would represent another massive sequential jump. The critical question for investors and industry observers is whether AI infrastructure demand sustains this pace through the rest of the year, or whether the rate of acceleration begins to normalize. Thursday's numbers made no argument for the latter.
