# EU Launches €10 Billion European AI Champions Initiative

*Brussels aims to create globally competitive AI alternatives to US and Chinese systems*

BRUSSELS — The European Commission unveiled Thursday a sweeping €10 billion investment initiative to support European artificial intelligence companies, aiming to create globally competitive alternatives to dominant American and Chinese AI systems.

The European AI Champions Initiative includes compute infrastructure grants, talent retention programs, regulatory sandbox zones where companies can test new AI products without full compliance burden, and coordinated public procurement frameworks to ensure European AI companies have access to government markets.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced the program at a tech summit in Paris, calling it "the moment Europe decides to lead in AI rather than follow."

"Europe has the talent, the industrial base, and the values to be a global AI leader," she said. "What we lacked was the scale of investment and the willingness to back our champions. This initiative changes that."

The initiative comes as European AI companies have struggled to compete with American firms that have raised hundreds of billions in venture capital and public investment. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta have collectively invested over $200 billion in AI development, while the largest European AI company, France's Mistral AI, has raised approximately $2 billion.

Analysts noted that Europe's regulatory environment under the EU AI Act—considered the world's most comprehensive AI legislation—has created compliance costs that put European companies at a competitive disadvantage against less-regulated competitors.

The new initiative attempts to address this through "regulatory sandboxes" where AI startups can develop products in controlled environments before navigating full compliance requirements. France, Germany, and the Netherlands have been designated as initial sandbox hosts.

Critics, however, questioned whether €10 billion is sufficient. "The US is spending that in a single quarter," said Professor Henrik Müller of the Technical University of Berlin. "Scale matters in AI, and €10 billion is a statement of intent, not a serious competitive response."

The initiative still requires approval from EU member states and the European Parliament, a process expected to take at least six months.
